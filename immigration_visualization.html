📈 The Rise of Immigration as a Political Weapon

💡 Key Insight

In 1960, Kennedy and Nixon didn't mention immigration once. Not because immigrants didn't exist—they did. But because immigration wasn't politically useful yet. The "crisis" was manufactured when it became electorally advantageous.

🎭 The Framing Game: Same People, Different Story

💡 Key Insight

Notice how 2016 was heavy on THREAT framing ("illegal", "criminals", "gangs") while 2020 shifted to COMPASSION framing ("families", "children", "separated"). The immigrants didn't change. The political calculation did.

📜 What They Actually Said

1960s-1980s The Silent Era
[Silence]

Kennedy and Nixon debated the Soviet threat, Cuba, civil rights. Immigration? Not mentioned. Reagan talked about Soviet expansion, tax cuts, the economy. Immigration got a few sentences about migrant workers. The "crisis" hadn't been invented yet.
1990s First Mentions

Immigration enters the conversation but isn't yet a wedge issue. Candidates discuss it as a policy matter, not an emergency.

⏱️ The "Broken System" That Never Gets Fixed

The Real Story

Every generation of Americans has been told that THIS wave of immigrants is different. They're dangerous. They won't assimilate. They'll destroy the country.

The Irish were "drunken criminals." The Italians were "anarchists." The Chinese were "unassimilable." The Jews were "communists." The Mexicans are "rapists and drug dealers."

Every single time, the fear-mongers were wrong.

The data from 60 years of presidential debates shows something remarkable: immigration wasn't even discussed in 1960. Your grandparents weren't worried about it. The country didn't collapse.

What changed wasn't immigration patterns—those have been relatively stable. What changed was political utility. Someone discovered that "invasion" rhetoric wins votes.

Your great-grandparents were somebody's "immigration crisis."
Their grandchildren became "real Americans."
So will today's immigrants' grandchildren.
